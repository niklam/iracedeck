# @iracedeck/callout-settings

The Race Engineer callout opt-in registry (#1350). It is the only place a `calloutEnabled*` settings key is declared: every key, its PI label, its default and the callout id it serves live in one entry here, and everything else derives from it. Design record: `docs/superpowers/specs/2026-10-06-issue-1350-callout-settings-registry.md`.

**Zero dependencies — keep it that way.** Plain TypeScript, no `zod`, no `@iracedeck/*` package. `@iracedeck/settings` (which builds the schema from it) and `audio-scenarios` must not depend on each other, so the registry has to be a leaf both can reach (the same reason `callout-script` and `track-data` are leaves). If this package seems to need something from another one, that something belongs here or the need belongs in the consumer.

## Modules (`src/`)

- `define.ts` — the shapes and typed helpers. `CalloutEntry` (`key`, `label`, optional `description`, optional `default`, optional `requiresFeature`), `CalloutFamily` (`id`, `callouts`), `defineCalloutFamily` (identity at runtime; its `const` type parameter keeps every id and key a literal type), `CalloutIdOf<F>` and `CalloutKeyOf<F>` (distributes over a union of families), `calloutKey(family, id)` (typed as exactly that id's key; an unknown id, which only an unchecked value can reach, throws naming the family and the id) and `calloutIdForKey(family, key)`.
- `families/*.ts` — one file per family, 42 in all, each exporting `<PREFIX>_CALLOUTS`. A family is named for the `audio-scenarios` family it serves, and its object keys are that family's callout ids. Six small families (`pit-service-requests`, `setup-warning`, `race-engineer-toggle`, `corner-names-toggle`, `telemetry-connect`, `fuel-countdown-sessions`) hold the keys no scenario family reads; they are named for what reads them. Two of those are family-wide gates over another family's contracts: `pit-service-requests` over every toggle confirmation, and `fuel-countdown-sessions` (#1702) over the `fuel` countdown, one opt-in per session kind (Race, Practice, Qualifying, Test) that `registerPitCrew` asks for the kind `getSessionClass()` names. Entries are in PI order, and the rationale that used to sit on each schema field sits on its entry.
- `pi-groups.ts` — `CALLOUT_PI_SECTIONS` (#1700): the seven collapsible sections of the settings window's Callouts card, each holding its headings (`CalloutPiGroup`: `id`, `title`, a required `description`, an ordered list of families), 31 headings in all; `CALLOUT_PI_GROUPS` is derived from it, the sections' groups concatenated in render order, and `RegisteredCalloutPiGroup` is any heading placed in a section, with its literal families.
- `index.ts` — the public surface: everything above (each family re-exported by name), plus `CALLOUT_FAMILIES` (every family, in PI order, derived from `CALLOUT_PI_GROUPS` rather than listed), `CalloutSettingKey` (the union of every key, derived), `RegisteredCalloutFamily` (`RegisteredCalloutPiGroup["families"][number]`, any family placed in a heading: a generic helper that must yield a `CalloutSettingKey` constrains on it, since `CalloutKeyOf` of a bare `CalloutFamily` is only `` `calloutEnabled${string}` ``), `CALLOUT_SETTING_KEYS`, `calloutEntry(key)` and `calloutDefault(key)`.

## The entry and its default

`default` is optional and can only be `false`: absent means on. New Race Engineer functionality ships on, so an off default is a visible, deliberate exception rather than a value every entry repeats. Today nine entries carry it: the fuel countdown counts 10, 9, 8, 7, 6 and 4, the two pit-lane beeps, `calloutEnabledPitExitCue` (#1161) and `calloutEnabledLimiterEngagedCue` (#1272), which the maintainer decided on 2026-11-09 a driver opts into, and the rejoin traffic warnings, `calloutEnabledTrafficRejoin` (#1113), which he decided on 2026-11-20 ship experimental and off, the label saying "(experimental)". `calloutDefault(key)` turns it into the schema default.

`requiresFeature` names the platform feature flag (`PlatformFeatureFlags`, `platform-features.json`) an entry's checkbox needs: the settings window renders the row only in a build whose flags have it `true`. It is for a callout built but not shipped; the key stays in the schema with its default either way, so nothing a user stored is lost. One entry carries it: `calloutEnabledTrafficRejoin`, `trafficRejoin` (the #1113 spec's fourth amendment, 2026-11-21: the rejoin warnings do not ship, so no release build shows the checkbox). The registry stays dependency-free, so the flag is a plain string; `registry.test.ts` pins which entries carry one, and `audio-scenarios`' traffic test that the catalog's development feature names the same flag.

A `key` is a persisted settings key and a published contract: never rename or drop one without a migration, and never change a default casually. `@iracedeck/settings`' append-only baseline (`src/__fixtures__/callout-settings-baseline.json`, #1376) fails on either. A key retired on purpose keeps its baseline row, marked `"retiredBy": "#<issue>"` and listed in the test's `RETIRED_KEYS` by the change that adds its migration, and is never reused.

## Families versus PI groups

A family is the unit of ownership: the ids are family-scoped (`ack` is an id of both toggle families, `speeding` of both `pit-limiter` and `no-limiter`, and `furled`, `black`, `meatball` and `disqualify` of both `flag` and `opponent-flag`), so the family is what `audio-scenarios` derives its id types from. A PI group is a heading in the window. Most groups hold one family; seven span several (Pit Limiter, Pit Service, Lap Time, Race, Gaps, Pit Speeding, Corner Names and Sectors), and a group's rows are its families' entries concatenated in list order. Every family belongs to exactly one group, and every group to exactly one section: a group is declared only inside a section, and `registry.test.ts` holds group ids unique across all sections, pins every section's headings in order, and keeps section ids apart from group ids. Headings within a section run in the order their calls come in a session or a stop.

## Descriptions (#1700)

Every heading carries a `description`, required by the type, and an entry carries one only when its label says neither what the engineer says nor when ("Crossed flags", "Two to green", "Can't fix that"; thirteen today). The settings window shows a heading's under its title and an entry's as a second line under its label. Each is one sentence for the driver, on one line, ending in a full stop, at most 160 characters for a heading and 120 for an entry: when the engineer speaks and what the engineer tells you (never "he": a voice pack brings its own voice), second person, plain words, US spelling. Never an internal name (a settings key, a family or callout id, an event name, "telemetry", "SDK", an issue number), never a quoted voice line (a pack scripts its own wording), and never the title or label restated as its opening words. Check each sentence against what the call does — its family comment, the scenario contract that gates on its key in `audio-scenarios`, and the translator condition behind it — before writing it; Niklas approves the text as user-facing. `registry.test.ts` holds the mechanical rules; the style is the #1700 spec's (`docs/superpowers/specs/2026-11-15-issue-1700-callout-sections-and-descriptions.md`).

**A key's spelling does not say which family owns it.** `calloutEnabledFlagOnePaceLapToGo` is the `rolling-start` family's `one-pace-lap-to-go` entry, labelled "One lap to green", since #1167 moved it there from `flag`: the call became a formation call, so its checkbox renders under Rolling Start beside the other two. The key keeps the `Flag` in its name because it is a stored setting and a rename would need a migration, and the scenario it gates keeps its id (`pit-crew.flag-one-pace-lap-to-go`) because every voice pack scripts it by that name. Moving an entry between families changes where its row renders and which family's id type carries it, and nothing a user has stored. It is also the one edit an existing baseline row has ever had: the row's `owner` moved from `flag/…` to `rolling-start/…`, with the key and its default untouched.

## Consumers

- `@iracedeck/settings` builds a `GlobalSettingsSchema` field for every `CalloutSettingKey` with `.default(calloutDefault(key))`, and exports the typed lookup `isCalloutEnabled(key)` and write `setCalloutEnabled(key, enabled)`.
- `audio-scenarios` derives each family's callout id type with `CalloutIdOf<typeof X_CALLOUTS>` and gates through `isCalloutEnabled(calloutKey(X_CALLOUTS, id))`.
- `pi-components` renders the settings window's Callouts card from `CALLOUT_PI_SECTIONS` (sections, then headings with their descriptions, then rows with theirs), with `default="true"` exactly where `calloutDefault(key)` is true, leaving out a row whose `requiresFeature` flag the build does not have on.

## Adding a callout

Add an entry to its family here, with a `description` when its label says neither what is spoken nor when (*Descriptions* above). When the family is new, that is a new family file, its place in one group inside a section of `CALLOUT_PI_SECTIONS` (which is what registers it: `CALLOUT_FAMILIES` is derived from the groups) and its re-export from `index.ts` (`registry.test.ts` fails on a placed family the index does not export). The schema field, the PI row and the id type follow from it. One manual edit follows too: a new key appends a row at the end of `@iracedeck/settings`' `src/__fixtures__/callout-settings-baseline.json` (#1376), written from the entry you declared rather than generated, plus the new pin `callout-settings-baseline.test.ts` prints — that test fails on a registry key with no row and on any pinned row deleted, swapped, reordered or re-defaulted, and its header gives the row's shape. Moving an entry to another family updates its row's `owner` and nothing else (#1167).

A new heading is a group, with its `description`, inside one section of `CALLOUT_PI_SECTIONS`, at the place in that section where its calls come; it needs no edit to the settings window's partial unless it carries a slider or a supporting text, which goes in the partial's map under its group id. A new section is a design decision, not a step of adding a callout: propose it with the issue that needs it.
