# A translator step's failure costs only that step

> **Issue:** [#1559](https://github.com/niklam/iracedeck/issues/1559) · **Supersedes:** _none_ · **Superseded by:** _none_
>
> Point-in-time design record. The code and `.claude/rules/` are the truth; this is not documentation.

Measured at `97e315452`. Line numbers are `packages/sim-events-iracing/src/translator.ts` unless named otherwise. Depends on #1558 (`docs/superpowers/specs/2026-10-31-issue-1558-sdk-subscriber-isolation.md`), whose bounded reporter and `ErrorPolicy` type this uses and whose subscriber boundary stays the outer one.

## The problem

`handleTick` (:2277) runs about forty steps per tick. The live diffs, past the replay guard, push their events into `pending` (:2650-2653), and `pending` is published only at the end (:3054-3056). Every diff advances its own baseline as it runs, so a throw anywhere between :2668 and :3052 loses the events of every diff that already ran, for good: on the next tick their baselines say the change has been seen. A lap, a flag or a finish is lost because the radar threw. The pre-guard steps (:2498-2600) publish directly, so their events survive, but a throw in one of them still skips everything after it. #1558 keeps the controller and the other subscribers running when the translator throws, and deliberately leaves this to the translator.

Two parts of the tick already behave correctly under a throw and are kept as they are. The replay guard's entry and exit (:2615-2647) set `lastTickInReplay` only after the teardown and the wipe, so a throw in either repeats the edge on the next tick. The session-identity block (:2349-2486) publishes `session.changed` and only then advances `lastObservedSessionNum` / `lastObservedSubSessionId` (:2474-2486), and the fresh-connect synthesis latches `freshConnectFireChecked` only after its publish, so a throw there retries the change on the next tick.

What can throw is mostly a resolver reading a session YAML of an unexpected shape. `getSessionInfo()` itself does not throw on a bad YAML: `IRacingSDK.getSessionInfo` (`iracing-sdk/src/IRacingSDK.ts:203-234`, #1461) catches a parse failure and caches `null` for that update, and the controller has already called it before the translator runs (`SDKController.ts:215`, the replay-state step). A YAML that parses into an odd shape is different. `resolveIsMultiClass` reads `driver.CarIsPaceCar` on each `Drivers` entry without `?.` (:3231-3232), so one `null` entry throws; `resolvePlayerResultsSnapshot` has the same exposure on `Sessions` and `ResultsPositions` entries (:3194, :3202). Such a throw repeats on every tick of that session-info update, and an update can begin or end mid-session.

## Decision

1. **The unit is a step: one call in `handleTick`, named by its function.** Every row in the tables above is a step, the ones that publish nothing included. The frame (F0 to F3) is not split. F1 and F2 rewrite the state every later step reads, so running the steps on a half-reset state is worse than losing the tick, and both already retry correctly. F0 and F3's read are controller calls whose parse failure is already `null`. A throw in the frame leaves `handleTick` and is caught by #1558's subscriber boundary, which reports it under the translator's subscription id.

2. **A step is all or nothing; a step over independent cars is all or nothing per car.** Each step runs inside one boundary, its argument expressions included, so a throw while computing an argument costs that step and no other. A live step pushes into `pending` as today; the boundary records `pending.length` before the step and truncates back to it on a throw. A pre-guard step emits into one scratch list that the boundary publishes when the step returns, and clears on a throw (`diffFirstOnTrack` takes an `emit` like the others). Either way a step that throws publishes nothing that tick, and every later step still runs. All-or-nothing is chosen over publishing what a step emitted before it threw because pairs come from one step: `lap.completed` and `race.finished` from `diffLaps`, `incident.occurred` and `incident.scored` from `diffIncidents`, and the #1211 yield reads the timestamp the second carries for the first. Half a pair is a worse tick than none. The failing step stays in and is called on the next tick like any other.
   - **Per-car units.** `tick-steps.ts` exposes the same boundary for a unit inside a step: a unit's events commit when it returns, a throw drops that unit's events only and is reported under the step's id, and the step's other units commit. A diff that loops over independent cars runs each car as its own unit, with that car's baseline written last inside the unit. Without it, step-level all-or-nothing would lose more than today for `diffReplayLaps`, which writes each car's baseline as it goes and publishes each crossing directly (`diff/replay-laps.ts:156-226`): a throw on car 30 would now discard the crossings of cars 0 to 29, whose baselines have moved on. The units apply to the per-car loops of `diffReplayLaps` and `diffReplayIncidents` (`diff/replay-incidents.ts:237-247`, and its player-count publish as one more unit), to `diffOpponentPit`'s per-car detection and classification (`diff/opponent-pit.ts:182-195`), and to `diffOpponentFlags`' per-car store advance and qualification loops (`diff/opponent-flags.ts:617-655`, :674-…). Field-wide work after a loop stays in the step: `diffOpponentPit`'s emit loop and aggregation (:205-230) and its baseline advance (:231-240), and `diffOpponentFlags`' burst aggregation, all share episode state across cars. For the two opponent diffs the units are about a persistent fault rather than a lost event (`diffOpponentPit` advances its baselines after the loops, so a throw in classification is retried), since one car with an odd row would otherwise silence the whole field. `diffReplayGreens` and `diffReplaySessions` emit at most one event per tick and loop over no cars, so their unit is the step.
   - Truncating is safe because no step reads its own events from `pending`: the three readers look only for other steps' events (`diffPitReadback` for `pitLane.approaching` and the toggle events, `diff/pit-readback.ts:145-215`; `updateStallVisit` for `diffPitLane`'s edges, `diff/stall-visit.ts:93-108`; `diffTireWear` for `pitLane.approaching` and the exit readback, `diff/tire-wear.ts:127-138`). A later reader therefore never sees an event a failed step pushed. It can still see state a failed step wrote, which is decision 4.
   - The direct steps still publish in the order they run; they now publish when the step (or unit) returns rather than mid-call, so a bus handler sees that step's state finished. No handler depends on the mid-step timing.

3. **A value that throws takes its last value this session; only with none does it take its absent-session-info answer.** Each value in the tables is resolved in its own boundary, once per tick (`resolveIsMultiClass` and `resolvePaceCarIdx` are hoisted to one read each, which changes no answer).
   - **Why the last value.** The fault is a YAML of unexpected shape, which lasts for the whole session-info update and can start mid-session. Mid-session the "unknown" answer is wrong rather than neutral: `isMultiClass = null` sends `diffOvertakes` to overall positions (`diff/overtakes.ts:193`, the phantom calls #588 fixed), and the opponent-pit and opponent-flag gates (:2890, :2915) then announce cars of other classes; `resolveCollisionCarValue(null)` speaks the pavement 4x value for a dirt 2x collision (`diff/incidents.ts:166-168`). And the diffs that advance a baseline while their gate is closed lose edges for good when an unknown session type closes it: `diffPitsOpen`; `diffFlags`' final-lap call (`diff/flags.ts:625-635`, so `playerFinalLapStarted` never sets) and its checkered finish record (:599); `diffLeaderWhite` (`diff/leader-white.ts:161-183`); `diffOpponentPit` (`diff/opponent-pit.ts:174-175`); `diffStartLights` (`diff/start-lights.ts:139`); and `diffPitLane`, whose dirt-oval entry edge (`diff/pit-lane.ts:123`) is skipped under `TrackType.Unknown` while the baseline still advances (:151-153). The last value is what the same session reported a moment ago, which is right for every one of these. It is the pattern three resolvers already follow for absent session info: `resolvePitSpeedLimit` (:3311), `resolveTrackLengthMeters` (:3257) and `resolveCornerMarkersCached` (:3287) return their cached value.
   - **Where it lives.** One store on the translator instance, beside `lastObservedSessionNum` / `lastObservedSubSessionId`, holding each value step's last good answer, so it belongs to the session F1 identifies. `resetPerSessionState` and `handleDisconnect` clear it; the replay wipe leaves it, as the session is the same.
   - **With no last value this session** (a fault from the first tick of a session, the connect window) a value takes the answer its resolver gives for absent session info, the state every diff already runs in before the session YAML arrives: `resolveSessionType` → `""` (so `isRaceSession` is false), `resolvePlayerCarIdx` → `-1`, `resolvePitSpeedLimit`, `resolveTrackLengthMeters` and `resolveCornerMarkersCached` → their cached values, `resolveTrackType` → `TrackType.Unknown`, `resolveIsMultiClass`, `resolvePitBoxTrkPct`, `resolveStartingGridPosition` and `resolvePaceCarIdx` → `null`, `resolveCollisionCarValue` → the pavement value.
   - **`resolvePlayerResultsSnapshot` keeps `null`, never its last value.** `diffLaps` reads `null` as "the standings have not caught up, wait" and defers the emit with its timeout, which is the honest answer; a last snapshot would hand it the previous lap's standings as this lap's.
   - **`resolvePlayerIsLeader` falls back to `false`.** The winner grace it feeds is a safety net (#771); `false` leaves the checkered to the player's own crossing, the normal path. It is read before the order is rebuilt this tick (one tick stale by design), and without its own boundary a persistent order fault would cost `diffFlags`, the safety callouts, in every race.
   - **The canonical order has no fallback, so its consumers are skipped.** It is unavailable for the tick when `updatePositionTracking` or `resolveCanonicalOrder` throws, since an order computed from half-written anchors is not the canonical one. Then `diffCaution`, `diffOpponentPit`, `diffOpponentFlags`, `diffLeaderWhite`, `diffOvertakes` and `diffGaps` (with `resolveGapLapsRemaining`) are not called that tick. `diffFuelLapsLeft` reads the order only through its leader-lap closure, whose designed answer for "no leader" is `null` (the player's own average is the fallback, #880), so it runs and the closure answers `null`. A skipped step advances no baseline, so a diff that compares the tick with its own baseline sees the change on the next tick it runs, as it would after a frame the poll never delivered. The order is not given the last-value treatment: it is computed from this tick's telemetry, and a last order would present the field as it was while the traces move on.
   - **`stallVisit` has no fallback either**, so `diffTireWear` is skipped when `updateStallVisit` throws.
   - **A value read by one step only, from telemetry, is part of that step**: `resolveGapLapsRemaining` belongs to `diffGaps`, the two closures to `diffFuelLapsLeft`, `classifyLapSessionType` to `diffLaps`.
   - A skipped step is not reported; the throw that caused it was.

4. **A step reading state another step writes still runs, on whatever that step left — with one same-tick flag turned into a value.** The translator does not track state dependencies. They are declared only in comments, so skipping on them would need a graph nobody maintains, and it would turn one persistent fault into several silent families (a `diffFlags` fault would take `diffLaps` and `diffFuelLapsLeft` with it). Most such reads already accept a one-tick-old value: `diffOpponentFlags` reads `diffGaps`' traces from the previous tick, and `diffFlags` and `diffStartLights` read the phase `diffCaution` settled the tick before.
   - **`fuelPlanChangedThisTick` becomes a value.** It is written by `diffToggles` (`diff/toggles.ts:269`, :343) and read by `diffPitReadback` (`diff/pit-readback.ts:215`) in the same tick, and a `diffToggles` throw before :343 leaves the previous tick's `true` in place, which fires a second readback. So `diffToggles` returns the flag, `handleTick` hands it to `diffPitReadback` as a value whose fallback is `false` (no change seen), and the state field is deleted (`state.ts:881`, :1916).
   - **The same-tick state reads this accepts**, each lost for every tick its writer throws: `diffLaps` reading `flagCheckeredTakenLap` (a `diffFlags` throw on the crossing tick misses the winner's finish, #1278); `diffGaps` reading `cautionPhase` (:2983; a `diffCaution` throw leaves the previous tick's phase, so a gap call can land on the tick a caution is thrown, #1171); and `diffFuelLapsLeft` reading `whiteLastLapFired` / `playerFinalLapStarted` (a `diffFlags` throw on the final-lap crossing leaves the fuel calls open on the last lap, #866). Each is a consequence of its writer's fault, which the reporter shows.

5. **What completed is published, in call order, whatever happens later in the tick.** The flush (F3) runs in a `finally` around the live half, so even a throw that escapes every step boundary (glue code between steps) publishes the committed events before it reaches #1558's boundary. Order on the bus is unchanged: a failed step leaves a gap and never moves another step's events. No consumer needs two events from different steps on one tick. The cross-step relations are orders, and a gap keeps each of them true: `flag.checkered.raised` before `race.finished` (:2681-2687), `limiter.dropped` never after `pitLane.exited` (`endsOn`, #1407, :2725-2733, and `TICK_ORDER`), `radar.changed` before `pitSpeeding.started` (#912, :3036-3042), and the exit readback before the tire-wear report (`queueBehind`, #1108, ordered again in the queue whether or not both arrive). The pairs that must arrive together come from one step and are dropped together (decision 2). `TICK_ORDER` (`tick-order.ts`) stays as it is; its `via: "direct"` comment now says the publishes reach the bus when the stage returns.

6. **The translator does not restore a failed step's state; keeping it consistent is the step's job.** What the translator promises: the step's (or unit's) events for that tick are discarded, the steps after it run, and it is called again on the next tick. It does not snapshot and restore `TranslatorState` around a step: the state is one object shared by every step, so nothing says which fields are the step's own, and a `structuredClone` of it forty times a tick at 60 Hz costs more than every diff together. What it leaves to the diff: a diff that wants a throw to be retried rather than lost writes its baseline last, after the work that can throw. That is the shape F1 and F2 already have, and since the step's events are discarded together with the throw, a diff written that way loses nothing. Existing diffs are not audited in this change beyond the per-car loops of decision 2 (Out of scope); a fault the reporter shows is fixed in its diff.

7. **Reports go through #1558's bounded reporter, injected, under `translator:<function name>`, with caps sized for the steps.** A step or value that throws is reported as `(translator:<function>, error)`: `translator:diffPitLane`, `translator:resolveCanonicalOrder`, `translator:updatePositionTracking`, the same names `TICK_ORDER`'s `stage` uses for a publishing step. A per-car unit reports under its step's id; the reporter's "where it threw" key keeps it apart from the step's other faults. The ids are a fixed set, so nothing needs forgetting. The reporter is an option of `initializeSimEventsIracing` (`SimEventsIracingOptions.errorReporter`, beside the existing knobs at :287). When it is omitted, the translator makes one with `createBoundedErrorReporter` from the logger it was given and `Date.now`, inside `initializeSimEventsIracing`, per instance, and drops it with the instance in `_resetSimEventsIracing`. Never at module scope: tests pass their own with a fake clock. It is the translator's own table, apart from the controller's, so the controller's caps cannot hide a translator step's first error or the reverse. #1558's global cap of 32 keys was sized for subscribers; the translator has about fifty step and value ids, so it passes a global cap of four keys per id it can report (`TRANSLATOR_REPORTER_MAX_KEYS`), keeps #1558's per-id cap of four, and the source check of decision 12 asserts the constant covers every id it finds. The global cap can then never be what hides one step's first error. The report call sits in its own `try`, so a logger that throws cannot cost the tick (the event bus guards its logger the same way, `event-bus.ts:73-77`). `@iracedeck/logger` is already a dependency, so no edge is added.

8. **The failure policy is injected, with #1558's shared type.** `SimEventsIracingOptions.onStepError: ErrorPolicy`, the `"report" | "rethrow"` type `@iracedeck/logger` exports beside `createBoundedErrorReporter`, default `"report"`, which every plugin build runs. Under `"rethrow"` every step still runs and the flush still happens, then the first error is thrown at the end of `handleTick`. `translator.test.ts` and the other suites that drive `handleTick` through a mock controller choose `"rethrow"`, so a diff that starts throwing in a translator-level test fails it instead of being reported and passing. A diff's own tests call it directly and stay loud without either.

9. **#1558's subscriber boundary stays the outer one.** It catches what this change deliberately does not: a throw in the frame, and anything rethrown under `"rethrow"`. The per-step boundary sits inside it and is the only boundary that knows a step.

10. **This holds whichever of #1468 and #1559 merges first.** The boundary is one module (`src/tick-steps.ts`: run a step, run a unit inside a step, read a value with a fallback, all with the injected reporter and policy), imported by every `handleTick` that runs steps. It stays in `sim-events-iracing` but imports nothing from iRacing (no `iracing-sdk`, no `TelemetryData`; it is generic over the event and the emit), so it can move to a shared home unchanged once a second translator exists. If #1559 lands first, the four replay recorders are pre-guard steps of the translator's `handleTick`, reported as `translator:diffReplay…`, and #1468's rebase moves them into `replay-recorder.ts` with their boundaries and units. If #1468 lands first, this change applies the same module to the recorder's `handleTick` too. Either way the recorder's four calls are steps reported as `replay-recorder:<function>`, it is handed the translator's reporter by `initializeSimEventsIracing` (one more argument to `startReplayRecorder`), and its direct publishes are released when each step or unit returns, as in decision 2. On `ir-1468` the recorder's frame is its `getReplayState()` read, its single `getSessionInfo()` read for the tick, the session-identity read, reset and advance, and the replay-edge reset after its four diffs (the `lastTickInReplay` branch, `replay-recorder.ts:256-262` there).

11. **The rules are written down.** `packages/sim-events-iracing/CLAUDE.md` gains a short section: a new call in `handleTick` goes through the step boundary under its function's name; a value it hands on gets its fallback stated in the same change (last value this session, else the absent answer, or its consumers listed as skipped); a diff looping over independent cars runs each car as a unit and writes that car's baseline last; no step reads its own events from `pending`; a same-tick flag one step hands another is a returned value, not a state field; a diff writes its baseline last.

12. **The boundary is enforced by a source check.** `tick-steps.test.ts` parses `translator.ts` with the TypeScript compiler API, finds `handleTick`, and asserts that every call statement in the pre-guard block and between the replay guard and the flush is a step, unit or value call through the boundary, the frame's calls named in an explicit allow-list. The same check walks the recorder's `handleTick` once #1468 has merged. Its positive control is a fixture source with one bare diff call, which the check must reject. It also collects the step and value ids it finds for decision 7's cap.

## Approaches weighed and rejected

- **Publish each live step's events the moment the step returns, and drop `pending`.** It would make the batch unnecessary, but a handler of an early event would then run before later diffs had seen the tick: the race-status `where:` reads the race-finished latch, `getLivePosition()` the order `updatePositionTracking` writes later in the tick. `pending` is what lets every handler see the whole tick, so it stays and only its losses are fixed.
- **Publish whatever a step emitted before it threw.** It keeps more events, and splits the pairs one step emits together (decision 2). The per-car units keep what that would have kept where the units really are independent.
- **The absent-session-info answer for every value** (the first draft). It rested on `getSessionInfo()` throwing, which #1461 already prevents, and mid-session it turns a shape fault into wrong calls and lost edges (decision 3).
- **Snapshot and restore `TranslatorState` around each step.** Too expensive at 60 Hz, and the shared state has no per-step ownership to restore by (decision 6).
- **Feed the canonical order's consumers the previous tick's order.** A stale order presented as current: under a persistent fault it freezes, and the gap and opponent lines then speak about the wrong cars with fresh traces. Silence for that tick is better than a wrong number.
- **Feed them `null` or an empty order.** None of their types takes one, and `diffCaution`'s comment (:2841-2844) records what a substitute does there: the leader lookup runs on the pace-lineup fallback, which disagreed with the running order for a whole lap in the capture.
- **Skip every reader of a failed step's state.** It needs a dependency graph the code does not declare and cascades one fault into many silent families (decision 4).
- **Remove a step after N throws.** Rejected for the same reason #1558 rejects unsubscribing a subscriber: one bad frame switches a feature off until restart, and N is a guess.
- **A `try` inside each diff module.** It works only where someone remembers it; the call site in `handleTick`, checked by decision 12, is the one place that covers every step.

## Out of scope

- Auditing each diff for writes made before work that can throw (decision 6), beyond the per-car loops named in decision 2. Faults are fixed in their diff as the reporter finds them.
- Hardening the resolvers against odd YAML shapes (the missing `?.` at :3231-3232 among them). The boundary makes such a fault cost one value; fixing each read is ordinary work once the reporter names it.
- Splitting the frame into steps.
- `handleDisconnect`'s teardown, which is not a tick.
- The getters other code calls between ticks (`getLiveRacePositions`, `getLivePosition`, `readSimState`): a throw there reaches its caller as today. `readSimState` already isolates its own halves.
- The event bus's handler isolation, which already catches (`event-bus.ts:63-79`); adopting the reporter there is #1558's follow-up.
- Changing `TICK_ORDER` or any diff's order.
- Showing step failures to the user.

## Testing

- **The boundary module** (`tick-steps.test.ts`), with a fake reporter: a step that returns commits its events; a step that emits two events and then throws commits neither, truncating `pending` to its mark; a step that throws is reported once as `translator:<name>` and the next step runs; a unit that throws drops its own events while the step's other units commit, reported under the step's id; a value step that throws returns its last value, and with none its fallback; a reporter whose logger throws costs nothing; under `"rethrow"` every step runs and the first error is thrown after the last; a pre-guard step's events reach the bus when it returns and not on a throw.
